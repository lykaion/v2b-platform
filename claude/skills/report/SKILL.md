# Reports

A report exists so the owner can stop reading his channels for a while and still not miss the
moment he is needed. So it opens with what needs him: questions put to him and still open,
decisions waiting on him, promises to or by him coming due, things going wrong that someone
expects him to know of. Already raised and still waiting comes before new, oldest first, with how
long each has waited: an item that has waited nine days reads differently from one raised this
morning. If nothing needs him, say that first; it is the most useful sentence a quiet report has.

Then what changed, business by business: decisions, things finished or slipped, figures that
moved, new people, disagreements, anything reversed. He cares more about the places he is active
in and the calls he attends than the channels he skips, but a decision made in a channel he
skips is still his business. A quiet business gets a line saying so, so he knows it was looked at.

Then what the report could not see: a failing source, a channel not copied, a gap in the period.
A report that silently skips a broken mailbox is worse than a short one.

Where to look: `stack/` first (look each open item up afresh, and clear what the copy shows
settled), `owner_involvement` for what is new, `summaries/` to see which channels were busy across the
days, then the messages themselves for anything you pass on. The period is what he asked for;
without one, since the last saved report under `reports/`, or the last day.

What makes it worth his time is judgement about what matters, not completeness: leave out
chatter, greetings and his own messages unless a reply to them waits. Every point stays
traceable in the way that fits it. Save the report under `reports/<date>-<time>.md`, and add
what you raised as needing him to `stack/`.
